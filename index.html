What it does

Rename by station

Reads the GPS out of each photo, finds the nearest station point in your KMZ, and writes a name you can sort — station, project, original filename. Videos too, on a machine that has ffmpeg installed.

Marco Polocator

Burns location, bearing, date and a caption onto the image itself, so the photo still says where it was taken after it has been mailed, pasted into a report and printed.

Google Earth KMZ

Exports the day as a KMZ, one file for each date folder. Every photo becomes a camera pin with a field of view showing which way the camera was pointed, and a plain camera where the phone recorded no heading. Click a pin and the photo opens in the balloon. Send it and the other person sees what you saw.

Stale GPS caught

A plausibility check catches coordinates that could not be right — usually a hot phone that stopped reacquiring GPS and stamped the photo with its last known fix. The suspect photos turn red in the preview, with the reason, before anything is renamed.

Portable

One executable and no installer. Settings, layers, profiles and tile cache live beside it, so it runs from a truck laptop and leaves the rest of the machine alone. The key binds to the first computer it opens on.

Who it is for

Environmental and utility inspectors who photograph a linear project all day and have to file every photo against a station.
